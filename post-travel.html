The New Safari Experience

A new generation of lodges is rewriting the safari, with slower days, smaller camps and a lighter footprint.

By Namna Staff · June 10, 2026 · 6 min read

The classic safari meant a crowded minivan, a checklist of animals and a race between parks. The new safari is slower, smaller and a lot more comfortable.

Fewer tents, more space

The best new camps have six to ten tents at most, spread far enough apart that you hear lions at night instead of your neighbours. Several sit in private conservancies next to the big parks, which means fewer vehicles at every sighting.

Slower days

Instead of dawn-to-dusk game drives, guests mix walking safaris, bush breakfasts and long afternoons by the pool. Guides are happy to spend an hour with one herd of elephants rather than chase a tick list.

A lighter footprint

Solar power, water recycling and tents that leave no trace when they are packed away are now standard at the better lodges. Many also share their fees with the communities who own the land, so staying there supports conservation directly.

Getting there

Most camps are a short flight from Wilson Airport, or a scenic day's drive from Nairobi. Travel between June and October for the dry season, or come in the green months for lower rates and newborn animals.

“Luxury out here is not gold taps. It is silence, space and a guide who knows every animal by name.” — Safari guide

Book early for the migration months, pack neutral colours, and leave room in your schedule to do nothing at all.
